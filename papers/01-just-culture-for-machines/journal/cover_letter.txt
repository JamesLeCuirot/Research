Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Just Culture for Machines: Why Penalising Disclosure Teaches Concealment", for publication in Safety Science as a research paper.

A long-standing lesson of aviation safety practice is that when reports of error feed punishment, people stop reporting and hazards stay hidden. Aviation's protected reporting arrangements (ASRS, ASAP, flight data monitoring, EU Regulation 376/2014 and ICAO Annex 19) are among its best-known answers. The manuscript argues that the training of AI systems now faces a similar incentive problem in a sharper form. Developers increasingly oversee AI systems through what the systems disclose about their own behaviour: their written reasoning, self-reports and "confessions". When those disclosures alone set the penalty for the behaviour they reveal, training can teach a system to hide misbehaviour rather than stop it, and experiments that trained reasoning models against chain-of-thought monitors have observed such concealment.

The paper maps the structure of aviation's protected reporting onto AI training. It states the conditions under which disclosure-only penalties cannot make good behaviour outscore concealed misbehaviour, and turns aviation's institutions into six design principles and five falsifiable predictions. It also sets out where the analogy fails. I believe it will interest Safety Science readers for two reasons: it applies a core safety-science concept to a technology entering safety-critical work, aviation included, and it shows that controlled experiments in AI training could test a mechanism that aviation could only observe.

The manuscript is original. It has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. An earlier version was posted as a preprint in the author's public research repository. Generative AI was used in its preparation, as declared in the manuscript, and the author takes full responsibility for its content. The author is the founder of CitadelAero, which builds AI tools for aviation; this is declared on the title page. The paper is a theoretical analysis and uses no data.
