# Screening calls flagged for audit

*The borderline decisions the screeners themselves flagged. These are the first reports for James and the independent checkers to re-screen, blind to any results. Ranks are draw ranks within each category.*

## A — unstabilised approach
- **The criterion 4 line.** An excursion was judged plausible only if the crew carried a high, fast, steep, long-landing or tailwind approach to touchdown, below the 500 ft gate. 61 of the 74 reports read failed on that line.
  - Ranks 19, 37, 67 and 73 sit close to it.
  - Rank 67 qualified mainly on a flaps-3 choice on a short runway.
- **Rank 6 (ACN 2225120) is not an approach case.** NASA's coding put it in A, but the crew's act was a rejected take-off at about 90 kt after mishearing a "stop" meant for another aircraft. It stays by rule, since categories come from NASA's coding, and it passes all six criteria. James to confirm it belongs.
- **Rank 62.** The captain's "blood alcohol equivalent" describes fatigue, not alcohol. The report failed criterion 4 anyway.
- **Ranks 21 and 23 are a likely duplicate** (TUL, July 2025). Both failed, so the duplicate rule never applied.

- **Replacement screening under D7, ranks 75–114:** 1 qualifier (ACN 2230585, rank 114), a thin pass on detail.
  - The line on criterion 4 was applied as before.
  - Closest fails: ranks 75 (late flaps), 79 (spoilers left out, consistent with the earlier rank 15) and 108.

## B — runway incursion
- **Ranks 9, 10, 14 and 23 failed criterion 4.** Someone else's incursion caused the go-around, and the crew made their own error afterwards, which could not cause a runway conflict. A stricter reading would fail them on criterion 1 instead. Either way the qualifier count is unchanged.
- **Rank 13 (SFO) failed criterion 1, the closest call.** The crew stopped for red runway lights after a crossing clearance, and the arriving aircraft went around.

## C — altitude deviation

- **Criterion 6, possible duplicate.** Ranks 11 and 17 are both an ERJ-145 at a masked airport in November 2025. Both were kept, because the local-time blocks differ and the narratives describe different actions.
- **Criterion 4, D3 recheck.** The before-and-after record is in `log_C.csv`; rows that failed the recheck start "D3 recheck:".
  - Two failed the recheck: 2201476 and 2305215, whose deviations happened low on final.
  - **Height above ground is mostly inferred,** because most reports give sea-level altitudes and no airport elevation. The closest calls kept are 2276020 (rank 3), 2307722 (rank 11), 2316309 (rank 6) and 2207565 (rank 19), the last with no height given.
- **Replacement screening under D7, ranks 28–33:** 3 qualifiers (ACNs 2287112, 2282213, 2300745).
  - Rank 30 failed the D7 cargo check, and rank 32 failed D3.
  - **Rank 28 (ACN 2287112)** depends on whether ATC actually cleared 10,000 ft. The report leaves it open; if so, it fails criterion 1.

- **Rank 26 (ACN 2211206) failed criterion 1.** The crew flew a correctly read-back ATC clearance below the minimum vectoring altitude. The first officer had doubts but did not query them.
