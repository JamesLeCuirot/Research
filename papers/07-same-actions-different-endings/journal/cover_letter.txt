Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Same Actions, Different Endings: Outcome Severity Shifts How AI Models Apply Reason's Culpability Decision Tree to Airline Flight Crews", for publication in Safety Science as a research paper.

Just culture asks that people be judged on what they knew and did at the time, not on how events turned out. General-purpose AI models are beginning to be used to read, code and triage occurrence reports, which puts them close to that judgement. This pre-registered, within-case experiment tested whether three current models judge identical flight-crew actions as more culpable when an occurrence ends badly. The cases are 48 real ASRS reports drawn by a fixed rule, each in two versions that differ only in the outcome, and the culpability outcome was derived in code from each model's answers to Reason's culpability decision tree.

All three models judged the crew more culpable after a bad ending. The effect was modest on average, concentrated in ambiguous cases, and absent in clear control cases, and it entered mainly through the substitution test. The models were also inconsistent across identical repeats. The paper draws practical implications for organisations considering AI in occurrence analysis, and offers a cheap matched-pair test they can apply to their own tools.

The study builds directly on work published in Safety Science, notably Oguz et al. (2026) on responsibility attribution by large language models in road safety, and on the hindsight and outcome-bias literature. I believe it will interest the journal's readers working on just culture, occurrence reporting and the use of AI in safety management.

The manuscript is original. It has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. The study was pre-registered, and an earlier version was posted as a preprint; the materials, data and code are openly available, with anonymised links given in the manuscript for review. Generative AI was used in the research and in preparing the manuscript, as described in the Methods and the declaration. The author takes full responsibility for the content. The author's company develops AI tools for aviation safety and compliance teams; no company product was tested, and this is declared on the title page.
