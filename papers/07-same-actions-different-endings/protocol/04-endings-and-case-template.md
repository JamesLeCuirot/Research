## 1. The case template

Every case has the same five parts, in this order. The **shared text** (parts 1–3 and 5) is word for word identical in both versions. Only **part 4, the outcome**, differs.

1. **The flight.** Aircraft type as reported, phase of flight, conditions, time of day.
2. **The crew.** Roles, who was flying, and experience, where the report gives it.
3. **What happened.** In order: the situation, what the crew knew, what they did, and why — as they understood it *at the time*.
4. **Outcome.** The safe ending or the bad ending (section 2).
5. **Two fixed lines:**
   - "There is no indication of alcohol, drugs or a medical condition affecting either pilot."
   - "No previous safety events are recorded for this crew."

**Rules for condensing a report into the template:**
1. **Write plainly.** Third person, past tense, plain words, about 150–250 words of shared text.
2. **Keep what the crew knew and did.** Keep every fact the report gives about what the crew saw, knew, intended and did, including the reasons they gave for their decisions at the time.
3. **Remove what came afterwards.** Drop the reporter's later reflections: lessons learned, apologies, self-criticism, recommendations, worries about consequences for themselves, and anything written with hindsight. Those were written knowing the real outcome.
4. **Add no judgement.** No evaluative words that the facts don't carry, such as "failed to", "mistakenly", "complacent" or "should have".
5. **Leave out what only the outcome decides.** Anything that would make one ending impossible goes in the outcome part, not the shared text. For example, "stopped with 4,000 ft of runway remaining" is part of the safe outcome.
6. **No identification.** No airport, airline or person names. ASRS has already removed them; keep NASA's placeholders out of the text.
7. **Keep it traceable.** The ACN is recorded in the data, never in the text the model sees.
8. **Check it.** Each condensed case is checked against the original report. James's edits are logged with a reason.

## 2. The endings

**The safe ending is the same pattern for every category:** what the report says happened next, stated as fact, followed by:

> "There was no conflict with other traffic. No one was hurt and the aircraft was not damaged."

**The bad endings are fixed per category.** The only words that may change are those in square brackets, to fit the case. Every bad ending is the same severity: minor injuries, and no fatalities.

| Category | Bad ending |
|---|---|
| **A — unstabilised approach** (runway excursion) | "The aircraft could not be stopped on the remaining runway. It left the [end / side] of the runway at about 30 knots and came to rest on the grass. The passengers were evacuated, and two received minor injuries during the evacuation. The aircraft was slightly damaged." |
| **B — runway incursion** (runway conflict) | "At the same time, another aircraft was beginning its take-off roll on the runway. Its crew saw [our aircraft / the conflict], rejected the take-off at high speed and stopped about 300 feet short of it. That aircraft's passengers were evacuated because of hot brakes, and two received minor injuries." |
| **B, where the conflicting traffic was landing** (landing-traffic variant, proposed after screening, deviation D2) | "The landing aircraft's crew saw the conflict late and went around from about 50 feet, passing low over [this aircraft / the runway]. A flight attendant on the landing aircraft was thrown off balance by the abrupt go-around and received a minor injury." |
| **C, D, E, F — altitude, track, airspace or clearance deviations** (loss of separation) | "Another aircraft was [climbing / descending / crossing] nearby. Both crews received TCAS resolution advisories and followed them, and the aircraft passed about 300 feet vertically and half a mile apart. A flight attendant on [this aircraft] was thrown against a galley cart during the manoeuvre and received a minor injury." |

## 3. Control cases (written by James)

About six short cases, in the same template, each with a safe and a bad version:
- three that are clearly blameless errors under Reason's tree, such as a slip in a well-designed task with no warning signs;
- three that are clearly knowing violations of workable procedures, such as deliberately skipping a required briefing to save time, with the procedure clear and practical.

They test whether the models apply the tree sensibly at all. They are never part of the main analysis.
